# Core feelings in roguelite, auto-battler, inventory, idle and loot games

Independent research, October 2026. A fresh reviewer with no knowledge of Black Bell chose the games, used web search (several
sites blocked full-page reads, so quotes are second-hand) and marked each point as sourced **[S]** or its own view **[Me]**.

## The feeling each game gives, in a player's words

| Game | Core feeling |
|---|---|
| Diablo III (after Loot 2.0) | "When something good drops, I drop everything." |
| Diablo IV | "I'm spending more time reading item stats than playing." (a failure case) |
| Path of Exile | "Everything I pick up might be a piece of my build, and I decide how." |
| Last Epoch | "I can aim at the item I want." |
| Vampire Survivors | "I'm tiny, then suddenly I'm a god." |
| Hades | "Even when I die, something moves forward." |
| Slay the Spire | "My deck is a machine I built, and I understand why it works." |
| Balatro | "The pieces click and the numbers go absurd." |
| Dead Cells | "It never feels cheap when I die." |
| Backpack Hero / Backpack Battles | "Packing my bag well is the game." |
| TFT / Super Auto Pets / Battlegrounds | "I made the plan, now I watch it play out." |
| Cookie Clicker / Antimatter Dimensions | "Numbers go up while I'm away, and each reset makes them go up faster." |

## Loot evidence

- **[S]** Path of Exile: Chris Wilson put items and collecting at the centre of almost every system; every currency item is
  useful and consumed at once; he avoids teaching "better colour means better item", so players inspect everything.
  ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/gdc-designing-path-of-exile)) Its designers admit most rare drops are skipped.
  ([Gamers Unchained](https://www.gamersunchained.com/news/path-of-exile-2-developers-discuss-item-design-and-mechanics-in-presentation-from-exilecon))
- **[S]** Diablo III: players "check the auction house first and play the game second"; Loot 2.0 aimed to make "the most fun way
  to play the game the best way to play the game". ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/83266/diablo-3-patch-201-deploys-with-loot-20))
- **[S]** Diablo IV: 200+ affixes, useless affixes on uniques and no chase items make drops dull.
  ([Wowhead](https://www.wowhead.com/diablo-4/de/news/the-problems-with-itemization-in-diablo-4-336951))
- **[Me]** Loot excitement depends on whether an item can change how you play right now. Shortcuts to the item destroy the
  feeling; stat choices players cannot read turn a find into homework.

## Shared core feelings, each with a test (all [Me])

1. **Earned surprise:** a random result changed my plan, and I did not buy or schedule it. *Can the player describe a surprise
   from one session?*
2. **I built this:** I can explain why my build works. *After a win, can the player name the 2–3 decisions that mattered?*
3. **Power I can see:** I am visibly and audibly stronger. *Could you tell from a muted recording?*
4. **Failure still pays:** a loss leaves me with something new, not just a smaller number. *What does the worst run leave behind?*
5. **Anticipation:** something good is possible soon. *Does the player name a chase item or milestone unprompted?*
6. **Fluent control:** difficulty comes from design, not friction. *When the player dies, do they blame themselves?*
7. **Light mastery:** I understand more each time. *Does the second run look measurably different from the first?*

## How they interact

- **Work together:** earned surprise with I built this (Balatro, Slay the Spire, PoE); failure still pays with anticipation
  (Hades); power I can see with fluent control (Vampire Survivors, Dead Cells).
- **Fight:** surprise against authorship when randomness overrides plans (Diablo IV unique affixes); anticipation against
  mastery when the chase is too rare to learn; visible power against anticipation once you are a god (solved by a new layer:
  prestige, new characters); loot volume against loot meaning.
- **Often claimed, rarely achieved:** every drop matters; meaningful choice; respect for your time alongside an endless chase;
  easy to start yet deep.

## Assumptions challenged (all [Me])

- Loot is about relevance (does this change my plan now?), not rarity and colour.
- More item depth is not better when players cannot read it.
- "Dopamine and gambling" is a thin explanation; understanding, synergy and visible power explain more.
- Roguelites rest more on choices and recovering from failure than on random draws alone.
- Idle games pull through progression and prestige structure, not idleness.

Evidence is thinnest for idle games and auto-battlers (reviews and summaries, no developer postmortems).
