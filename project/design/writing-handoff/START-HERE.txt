GAME DIRECTION

A medieval dark-fantasy dungeon game, inspired by the retro feel of Wizardry.
The player starts with a weak, poorly equipped crew and builds power through
items, upgrades and combinations. The intended game is 2D, side-view and native
landscape for mobile. Combat is an auto battler organised into rounds, with
interesting grid decisions between resolutions. The user wants a real game
layout and snappy touch interactions.

The equipment grid is central. Items occupy different-sized footprints.
Their placement, activation order, targets and combinations matter. Feedback
should show cause and effect: a potion flies over and pours onto a blade before
it attacks, then another item reacts. Item behaviours should be modular and
data-oriented. Appearance, family tags and character archetypes should not
prevent compatible interactions. Archetypes can emerge from items and crew
development rather than requiring fixed exclusive classes.

This remains a mechanics prototype. The playable demo uses black-and-white
placeholders; concept images are design references. The user prefers short
ideas, visual Draft iterations and discussion before locking a large design.

FIVE ESTABLISHED FAMILY DIRECTIONS

1. EARTH & GROWTH
Earth-elemental power, nature weaving and organic materials. Growth is the
philosophy. Druids, herbalists, healers and poison-makers fit here. Practices
include herbs/potions/poisons, mending/healing and spiritual or aura effects
through relics and totems. What spirits are, how people gain access and the
limits of growth are open. Emblem: rooted tree, broad leaves and a root stone.

2. MARTIAL / COMBAT
Traditional fighting through skill, strength, training and weaponry. Blades,
hammers, bows, close combat and ranged combat belong here, including warriors
and archers. The tone includes bloody, brutal violence. Weapon differences and
the broader philosophy remain open. Emblem: crossed blade, hammer and arrow.

3. SCHOLARSHIP / SCIENCE
Knowledge, mathematics, experimentation and scholarly practice. Chemistry,
alchemy and electricity are foundations to explore. Wealth and influence can
enable access to reagents, tools, research and expertise; their exact role in
combat, supply or campaign play is open. Alchemy can overlap with Earth &
Growth. Technology level and the relationship between science and magic are
open. Emblem: compass enclosing a flask with a lightning mark.

4. DARK / HUNGER
A mixture of dark human impulses and outside entities. Vampirism, appetite and
curses are central directions. Slow buildup can produce great harm to an enemy
or a powerful benefit to self. Manipulation and temptation remain possible.
What feeds a curse, when it pays off, its costs, expiry and entity nature are
open. Do not assume that curses automatically grow every round. Emblem: fanged
chalice with a blood drop.

5. SCAVENGING / COLLECTION
Resourcefulness and recognising value others overlook. Find useful goods in
junk; appraise rarity; collect, salvage and manage what the crew carries.
Scavenger, collector, packkeeper or quartermaster roles fit. Luck is optional.
This direction grew from a Fate/Fortune suggestion, but the user chose the
practical collection/storage emphasis. Discovery, rarity, collection rewards
and repair/crafting scope are open. Emblem: pack, rough shard and gem.

These are setting families, not five mutually exclusive character classes or
a final cosmology. No universal magical fuel, gods, guilds, history, morality
system or sixth family has been established. Suggested alternatives such as
Faith/Oaths or Dreams/Memory were not adopted.

ITEMS, GRID CONNECTIONS AND EFFECTS

Core interaction directions: Action, Preparation, Reaction and Storage.
Further ideas: Supply, Relay, Conversion, Amplification, Pattern and Timing.
Describe behaviours as trigger -> target -> effect -> limit. Separate what an
effect does from when it runs and how it chooses a target.

The user specifically confirmed storage extends the same connected grid to the
RIGHT by adding FULL-HEIGHT columns. Chains can cross into those columns. It
does not mean a disconnected mini-bag. Storage is modular across all families.
Exact expansion amounts, costs, stacking and removing occupied space are open.

Item sockets and empowerment are requested concepts. Socket types, quantities,
compatibility, costs and removal rules are not locked.

Shared effect ideas on the board:
- Act: damage, heal, mend, guard.
- Modify: empower, weaken, cleanse, apply a status.
- Produce/convert: create supply or spend ingredients to make another resource.
- Transfer/store: route charges or resources and keep them for later.
- Space/access: expand grid space, reveal properties or unlock access.

Possible targeting: touching neighbours, facing direction, rows, linked items,
sockets and bounded auras. Each item should show its targets. These are options
to explore, not universal rules that every item must use.

OUTSIDE COMBAT: LOOTING

The user corrected the word "looping" to "looting". Current notes are about
discovering, inspecting, carrying and using finds. No automatic workshop ticks
or offline production system was accepted.

Possible flow: discover -> inspect -> choose Keep/Place/Salvage. Preview affected
cells, results and costs. Preserve player choice before destroying a valuable
find. A full grid creates choices: rearrange, add storage, leave a find or
salvage something. Collection should not accidentally trigger combat attacks.

Family examples are proposals:
- Growth: identify organic finds; herbs + bottle + mortar could make medicine
  or poison, spending ingredients.
- Combat: open a cache with a tool; repair damaged loot; keep or salvage gear.
- Scholarship: inspect unknown materials or devices with a lens/assay tool.
- Hunger: capture remnants or inspect/contain a cursed relic; keep, cleanse or
  sacrifice it. Costs and risks remain open.
- Scavenging: sort selected junk into parts, recognise rare components and use
  pack space to carry useful goods.

CURRENT PLAYABLE MECHANICS — DISTINCT FROM DESIGN PROPOSALS

One recruit, one enemy at a time, a 3x3 grid, six items and three fixed fights.
The recruit starts with only the dagger equipped; all six items are available
to test. Each item activates once at its starting cell in left-to-right,
top-to-bottom order. Preparation targets the blade immediately to the right.
The tooth listens from round start and can react once per round to an
edge-adjacent blade's poisoned hit. Late preparation does not make the blade
attack twice. Presentation controls do not change combat outcomes.

- Rusty dagger: 1x2, 2 damage; consumes prepared venom and sharpening.
- Venom vial: 1x1, prepares +2 poison for the blade immediately right; refills
  each round in this prototype.
- Whetstone: 1x1, prepares +2 next-strike damage for the blade immediately
  right; expires this round.
- Scavenger tooth: 1x1, poisoned blade hit -> +2 guard, once per round.
- Buckler: 2x2, +3 guard for this round.
- Salve: 1x1, restores 2 health up to 12; refills each round in this prototype.

Poison deals its stack amount before the enemy and loses one stack. Enemy
attack is 4. Unused preparation and guard expire each round. Fights have enemy
health 14, 18 and 22. The next fight restores recruit health to 12 and keeps the
equipment layout. Retry also keeps the layout. Restart returns to a bare
dagger. There is no implemented loot/reward system, grid expansion, sockets,
crew progression, persistent save or procedural dungeon yet.

The user disliked "ring the bell" as the interaction theme. The main button is
Play round. Older Draft text and log messages still use bell language; this
does not establish a mandatory bell ritual or final title.
