## How level blockouts fail

Ordered by damage.

1. **Everything is a box with a hat.** This is the row that scores worst, every
   run, and it is the last one anyone fixes. A castle wall batters, so it is a
   frustum; a cliff is not a prism; a road bends and crowns; a mill house is a
   drum, not a cube with a cone on it; a hangar is a barrel vault. A cube needs
   a written reason - a crate is a cube, a shipping container is a cube, a
   modern office slab is a cube, and almost nothing else is. See
   [What a building actually is](#what-a-building-actually-is).
2. **The tray.** Everything on one rectangular ground slab with straight edges
   reads as a museum model on a base. Overlap several ground masses at
   different heights; break the boundary.
3. **Wedding-cake terrain.** Landform as stacked slabs. Make the main landform
   thick, step the tops to clearly different heights, vary every edge.
4. **A route in doubt.** Equal choices, nothing pulls, the player stands still.
5. **Clump and void.** Dense within a few metres of the hero, bare elsewhere.
6. **Floating, sunk, interpenetrating.** No render shows these. Check numbers.
7. **Review images that show nothing.** A camera jammed against a near wall
   renders a clean useless picture and will not say so. If you cannot name what
   a frame shows, the camera is wrong, not the level.
8. **Trusting your own score.** Rank passes against each other; never certify one.

`references/failure-modes.md` has the evidence behind each.
