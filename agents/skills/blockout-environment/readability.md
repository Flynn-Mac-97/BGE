## Readability tests - run all six, every pass

A whole-scene silhouette is a black rectangle. Do not render one; a level is
judged by its groups, its depth and its framing, not its outline.

1. **Squint.** Blur or shrink the shaded render hard. Do the big masses still
   read as separate groups? Porridge is a value problem, not a detail problem.
2. **Depth layers.** Count near, middle, far. Each sits at its own grey.
3. **Landmark alone.** Silhouette the hero against its close neighbours only.
4. **Skyline.** Does the top edge step, or run flat?
5. **The walk.** Five frames at eye height along the route, figure visible.
   Then answer the one question no still frame answers: **walking in, can a
   player tell where to go?** If not, the layout is unfinished.
6. **The gameplay frame - one per play area, from pass 2 on.** Render each area
   at the distance the game actually ships at, with the figure standing where a
   player would stand. Set it by the figure, not by a metre count: a 1.8 m
   character at roughly 8-12% of frame height is the Hades room / Stardew
   screen band, which lands near 20-28 m across the frame on a steep isometric.
   Put a second figure a few metres away so the space between characters reads.

   On a fixed-camera game this is the test, not an extra. An overview frame
   flatters everything: a level can score well on read, route, framing and
   scale at overview distance and be empty at the distance the player sees. One
   run learned the rule the hard way and it holds generally - **content that
   sits on the walked surface reads at gameplay distance; content at the rim of
   a space does not.** Its central court had all its pass-3 work around the
   edges, so at 22 m the middle was bare floor and the figure had nothing to
   measure against within 6 m. Check the same frame for occlusion: a tall mass
   in front of the play space hides the ground behind it, and a figure standing
   there reads as standing on its roof.

For an interior, hide everything above head height before the plan view - the
ceiling slab *and* the joists, ducts, lights, signs and cables. Miss the slab
and the plan view is a photograph of the roof.
