## Three things must be true as numbers, not opinions

None of them is visible in a render. Floating and interpenetrating masses
survived five passes and a full rubric on real builds; one village run had a
house inside its own wall.

- **Everything stands on something, and stands fully on it.** Contact is not
  enough: a mass's whole footprint must sit inside the outline of the surface
  it stands on - ground, a mass beneath, or a carrier you can name. A plinth
  with a corner over the edge of its terrace, a bench half off its deck, a
  lantern base overhanging a stair nose: every one of these passes a contact
  check, looks like a mistake at gameplay distance, and quietly kills the read
  of an otherwise good frame. Test containment, not just contact. Overhangs
  that are meant - a balcony, a cantilevered bridge, a jetty, a cornice - are
  legal, so keep a list of the ones you intended and let the check flag
  everything else.
- **Two physical things do not share space** unless the join is real.
- **The route is clear.** Sweep the player's capsule along the path you intend
  and prove nothing intersects it. Doors, stairs, gaps and cover all have to
  pass. A corridor 0.2 m too narrow looks perfect and plays broken.

Satisfy yourself by whatever means convinces you, and be suspicious of a check
that never complains. Prove it bites: move one mass 1 m into the path and
confirm the check screams.

## Level-design craft the masses have to carry

- **A landmark, framed from the entry.** David Shaver: the player sees it
  through a portal or a gap, with ground detail leading toward it. Composition
  is set at the blockout, from the beginning (Andreas Lohse); it is not added
  later.
- **Clear distinct groups the player can measure and memorise** (Robert Yang).
  A place you can describe to a friend is a place you can navigate.
- **Elevation is information.** Height says what is important, what is
  dangerous, and where you have already been.
- **Chunky cover.** Thin slivers and small gaps read as noise and play badly.
- **Pacing.** Alternate tight and open. A level of one width is a tunnel; a
  level of one openness is a field.
- **Every metre the player crosses earns its screen space.** Dense near the
  hero and bare everywhere else is the commonest scene failure.
- **Asymmetry is the mechanism.** Push each mass out past its neighbour on one
  side only. Rotation is the finish, not the fix. Grid maps are the exception,
  and they buy it back with elevation and prop placement.
