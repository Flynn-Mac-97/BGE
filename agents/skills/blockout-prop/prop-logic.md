# Prop logic, and where scale comes from

### Prop logic

- Moving parts clear their whole travel: blade, lid, door, hammer, bolt
- Every part fixes to another at a joint you can name
- Parts turn about their real pivot - hinge, axle, hub - not the mesh centre
- Real machines go asymmetric where their job does; mirror nothing the reference doesn't
- A screw holds **one** piece: inside its outline, inset from the edge, regular
  pitch, never on a joint. A seam gets a gap or a step, not a fastener
- Vehicles: panel breaks and stance first - ride height, rake, track width,
  wheel-to-arch gap. They are the read

### Scale

Proportion is in the drawing and is ground truth - a form you can see whole is a
ruler for everything else. Absolute scale is not in the drawing at all. Anchor
on something whose real size you know: grip ~0.11 m, knob 0.015-0.03 m,
pushbutton 0.010 m, toggle 0.03 m, cable 0.008 m thick, screw head 0.005 m,
inspection window 0.15-0.25 m. State which you used and what you assumed.

Scale is one number and the cheapest thing to fix. Never distort a mass to hit a
measurement - get the shape right, then set the scale once, by eye, at the end.
