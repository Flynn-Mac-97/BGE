# The method

**Stop, Limit, Compare** (JaviSkau), in that order.

- **Stop** - look and measure before modelling. Most of the job. Feels like delay.
- **Limit** - four greys only: `10 / 30 / 70 / 90`, ground 50, no mass at 50.
  Value is the strongest contrast (Blevins) - the eye separates shapes by
  lightness first, so touching masses sharing a grey stop being two masses.
- **Compare** - a 1.8 m figure in the scene from the first mass
  (Liittschwager, Sony Santa Monica). A size invented with nothing real beside
  it drifts, and drifts further with every part stacked on top.

**Keep it rough and loose** (Vaccaro, Naughty Dog) - the feel changes once art
goes in, so a blockout that is easy to edit beats one that is precious.

### Tiers

| Tier | Contains | Test |
|---|---|---|
| primary | the few big masses carrying the read from across the room | you could name the object from these alone |
| secondary | forms sitting on primaries - handles, modules, panels, wheels | remove it and the object is plainer but still recognisable |
| tertiary | hand-scale cues - knobs, screws, ports, vents, latches | only visible up close |

Blevins's proportion rules, broken only on purpose: split **70/30, never
50/50**, because two equal halves give the eye nowhere to settle; each tier
~30% the size of the one above; vary sizes within a tier, or a row of identical
parts reads as texture; ~70% detail to 30% rest, so the busy areas register.

### Modes

- **rough** - stop when pass-1's gate passes. Named by "rough", "quick",
  "low-fi", "prototype", "base shapes", "just the major forms".
- **full** - passes 1-3 plus two or three refinement edits. The default.

Decide out loud before starting. Ask once only if genuinely ambiguous; after
that nobody answers, so decide and proceed. Rough is an earlier stop, not a
lower standard: the primaries get the same treatment either way.

### Passes - cut first, add second

| Pass | Cut into what exists | Then add |
|---|---|---|
| pass-1 | nothing yet | primary masses |
| pass-2 | reveals, seams, wells in the primaries | secondaries |
| pass-3 | recesses in the secondaries | tertiaries |

A primary that is the wrong size or facing drags every child with it - wrong at
six masses is free, wrong at eighty is a rebuild. Detail cut into a mass reads;
detail beside it does not. One build added 48 lumps beside forms instead of
cutting in and scored 0.4/10.

Count what the reference actually has before deciding what you have. A
professional camera-prop blockout ran to 214 parts because the camera had that
many lugs, latches and brackets - evidence, not a target.

### Gates - a tier does not land until it passes

Bevel and light before every render, both modes. An unpolished box catches no
light on any edge, so it reads as a diagram of a form and the gate judges a lie.
Finish the mesh before the *first* frame, not before the hero - one run bevelled
after its orthos and viewmodel, so three of six gate frames were judged
unbevelled for three passes and nothing complained.
Keep hard edges sharp - a 40° smooth melts a chine, a knurl tooth or a deck
split you need.

| Gate | Asks |
|---|---|
| pass-1 | scale right, outline names the object, every mass faces its use, nothing floats |
| pass-2 | cuts read as recesses not lines, secondaries meet primaries at a named joint, 70/30 holding |
| pass-3 | density matches the reference, fixtures legal, thumbnail still reads |

Fix failures inside the pass. An error carried up a tier costs every part above it.

Two things must be true as numbers, not opinions: **everything stands on
something** (ground, a mass beneath, or a carrier you can name - a bracket is
held by the flank it bolts to), and **two physical things do not share space**
unless the join is real. Neither is visible in a render; floating and
interpenetrating parts survived five passes and a full rubric on one build.
Satisfy yourself every pass by whatever means convinces you, and be suspicious
of a check that never complains.
