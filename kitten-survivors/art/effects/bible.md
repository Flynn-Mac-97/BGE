# The art bible for effects

Generated by the Art Direction plugin from `brief.json`, `references/` and `rulings.json` in this directory. Do not edit this file — edit those and run `art.bible`.

This covers **effects** only. The other subjects with their own bible: `interface`, `world`.

## What it has to communicate

A weapon firing, a rat dying and a gem landing must each be legible in a frame holding a hundred enemies. Effects are the whole feedback loop: a hit nobody sees is a game with no combat.

## The camera that sees it

Same top-down 60 degree view as the world. An effect is 0.2 to 1 m across and lives under half a second, so it is a few dozen pixels on a phone for a few frames.

## The questions this direction answers

- How does an effect stay separable from the actors it is drawn over?
- How bright must a hit be to read against a bright ground?
- How long can an effect last before a hundred of them become fog?

## The rulings

These have a number, and `art.check` tests every one of them against a frame.

### A frame with a hit in it contains something near white.

Those three measure 0.949, 0.849 and 1.000. A hit is read as a flash, and a flash that is not the brightest thing on screen is not a flash. This is the one thing allowed to outshine the cat.

**Checked:** `value.p95` at least 0.85. Run `art.check` against a frame.

**From:** `holocure.jpg`, `20-minutes-till-dawn.jpg`, `brawl-stars-combat.png`
### Effects take the full chroma nothing else is allowed.

Those measure 1.000, 0.901 and 1.000. The world sits low in saturation on purpose, so full chroma is a channel nothing else uses and an effect is separable by colour alone.

**Checked:** `saturation.p95` at least 0.88. Run `art.check` against a frame.

**From:** `cult-of-the-lamb.jpg`, `vampire-survivors.jpg`, `brawl-stars-combat.png`
### Effects stay sparse. The screen never becomes noise.

Those four run 0.009 to 0.057. Vampire Survivors measures 0.190 and is the failure this bound exists to stop: past that the frame is noise and the player cannot find their own character. It is in the shelf as the negative case and is deliberately not cited here.

**Checked:** `edgeDensity` at most 0.09. Run `art.check` against a frame.

**From:** `holocure.jpg`, `20-minutes-till-dawn.jpg`, `brawl-stars-combat.png`, `cult-of-the-lamb.jpg`

## Rulings that follow from the format

No reference produced these and no frame tests them. They follow by arithmetic from the camera, the screen and the unit scale, so they hold whatever the style is and they outrank anything above that disagrees.

### No effect is smaller than 0.3 m across.

The cat is 0.8 m and about 5% of the frame, so 1% of the frame is roughly 0.16 m. On a phone about 1080 px on its short edge, a 0.3 m effect is near 20 px. Below that it is a coloured pixel nobody can identify, and it costs the same to draw.

**Derived.** It follows from the fact above, not from a picture. Nothing enforces it automatically.

## Rulings that need an eye

No measurement decides these. Check them with `art.compare` and a forced choice between two frames, never with a score.

### An effect is recognisable in the three frames it exists for.

An effect lives under half a second. Anything whose shape only resolves over a longer animation is decoration the player never sees.

**Judged, not measured.** No number decides this one. Run `art.compare` and read the two frames side by side.

**From:** `brawl-stars-combat.png`, `holocure.jpg`
