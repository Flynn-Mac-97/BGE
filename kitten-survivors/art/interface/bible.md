# The art bible for interface

Generated by the Art Direction plugin from `brief.json`, `references/` and `rulings.json` in this directory. Do not edit this file — edit those and run `art.bible`.

This covers **interface** only. The other subjects with their own bible: `effects`, `world`.

## What it has to communicate

Health, timer and level read at a glance without the eyes leaving the cat. Level-up cards are picked with a thumb. A child reads icons and numbers, never sentences.

## The camera that sees it

Portrait phone, about 7 cm wide, held at arm length. The HUD overlays the game, so every pixel it takes is a pixel of arena the player cannot see.

## The questions this direction answers

- How much of the screen may the HUD take before it eats the game?
- How large is a touch target a child can hit while panicking?
- How does a HUD stay readable over a bright moving world?

## The rulings

These have a number, and `art.check` tests every one of them against a frame.

### The HUD is bright. Something in it is near white.

All four references clear it, from 0.662 to 0.940. A HUD dimmer than the world it sits on is a HUD the player has to hunt for.

**Checked:** `value.p95` at least 0.66. Run `art.check` against a frame.

**From:** `archero.jpg`, `crossy-road.png`, `angry-birds-2.png`, `subway-surfers.jpg`
### HUD elements carry strong colour, not grey.

All four run 0.735 to 1.000. A child finds a red bar and a gold number. Grey chrome is an adult convention and disappears over a green field.

**Checked:** `saturation.p95` at least 0.73. Run `art.check` against a frame.

**From:** `archero.jpg`, `crossy-road.png`, `angry-birds-2.png`, `subway-surfers.jpg`
### The HUD is made of few large shapes, never fine detail.

All four sit between 0.028 and 0.057, a tight agreement across four unrelated games. Fine detail at arm length on a phone is noise, and noise over a moving world is worse.

**Checked:** `edgeDensity` at most 0.06. Run `art.check` against a frame.

**From:** `archero.jpg`, `crossy-road.png`, `angry-birds-2.png`, `subway-surfers.jpg`

## Rulings that follow from the format

No reference produced these and no frame tests them. They follow by arithmetic from the camera, the screen and the unit scale, so they hold whatever the style is and they outrank anything above that disagrees.

### Anything a player taps is at least 11% of the screen short edge.

The platform minimum touch target is 44 pt on a screen 390 pt wide, which is 11.3%. A child panicking in a horde is less accurate than an adult, not more, so 11% is a floor and not a target. On a 1080 px short edge that is 120 px.

**Derived.** It follows from the fact above, not from a picture. Nothing enforces it automatically.

## Rulings that need an eye

No measurement decides these. Check them with `art.compare` and a forced choice between two frames, never with a score.

### The HUD says things with icons and numbers, never sentences.

The game is for children, some of whom cannot read, and for a player whose eyes must return to the cat inside a second.

**Judged, not measured.** No number decides this one. Run `art.compare` and read the two frames side by side.

**From:** `crossy-road.png`, `subway-surfers.jpg`
### Every HUD element stays readable over the brightest part of the arena.

The world direction is a bright warm day. A HUD designed against a dark mock-up will vanish over lit grass, and this is only settled by looking at both together.

**Judged, not measured.** No number decides this one. Run `art.compare` and read the two frames side by side.

**From:** `archero.jpg`, `angry-birds-2.png`
